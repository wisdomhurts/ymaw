01 · The idea

Make the site do to a visitor what the weekend does to a young man.

The hero's journey has three moves: separation, trial, return. A young man arriving at ymaw.com on his phone, sent there by his father, is at the first move whether he knows it or not. The site should feel like that. Not a camp brochure with a price at the bottom, but a place that takes him seriously, shows him the real thing, asks him one honest question, and ends with an action he takes himself.

The current site is good and it is honest. It is also written from the parent's side of the screen, and it is built on a mix of real footage and animated photographs. The reimagined site keeps what works (the scroll-driven trail, the field-guide palette, the standards, the "young men, never boys" discipline) and changes three things at the root:

  • Two doors at the front. "I'm 12 to 17" and "I'm bringing him." Same weekend, two voices, one registration. The young man is never talked about in the third person on his own path.
  • Every frame is real. No animated stills, no stock, no AI. What is on the site happened at a YMAW weekend. This is the one thing a young man can smell from a mile off, and the one thing no other youth program's website in BC can claim.
  • The site crosses into the weekend. The media release he and his guardian complete at registration is what the men work from on Friday. The one thing the site says about Sunday, that he will be acknowledged by the men who watched him all weekend, is kept by the team, not by code. The website is the first station and the bus is the second.

We aren't a summer camp; we're a rite of passage.

YMAW enrolment call script

What "award-winning" actually takes, stripped of the hype: one clear idea executed all the way down, typography and motion that belong to the subject, real material, and a page that loads fast and works for everyone. Sites that win do one thing nobody else could do. Ours is that everything on it is true.

02 · Six rules

The standards the site is held to, the way the men are held to theirs.

  1. Real over rendered. Photographs and film from YMAW weekends only. Where we have no footage of something, we write about it or we film it in September. We do not generate it.
  2. His voice and theirs. Every page speaks to the young man in the second person somewhere, and to his family in the plain, unhurried voice a good production man uses at the bus stop.
  3. Nothing invented. No statistics we cannot source, no quotes nobody said, no promises the weekend does not keep. If it is on the site, a man on the team can stand behind it in a parking lot.
  4. Consent is the design constraint. The media release is part of registration itself: a required grant-or-decline choice with a typed signature, not a follow-up email. No identifiable young man appears without a grant on file. The site is designed so that it is beautiful with backs, hands, silhouettes and landscape, and becomes richer as grants arrive.
  5. The sacred stays off camera. Confidentiality is respected at the weekend, in Dorian's words. The Push, the Sunday ceremony and anything said in a circle are never filmed, recorded, quoted or described beyond what the handbook already says publicly. No quote on the site comes from a circle, ever.
  6. Fast, quiet, for everyone. Loads in under two seconds on a phone on a bus. Works without sound, without motion, without JavaScript, with a screen reader. Sound and motion are offered, never imposed; you opt in with a tap.

05 · Content

Two voices, one truth, written new.

All copy is rewritten from the source documents (The Big Why, the standards, the flyer, the partner templates, the communications strategy) and from Dorian's answers in section 10. Two registers:

To him
Second person, present tense, short sentences, no adjectives that sell. Says what is hard first. Never explains what he should feel. "No phones. Everyone survives this, including you."
To them
Plain, unhurried, specific. Safety and standards before magic. Names what $320 covers to the meal; $320 flat, no early bird. The Squamish region, never the site's name. Non-denominational, in that word. Ends every page with the one thing they can do next.
Quotes
Never from a circle. Confidentiality is respected at the weekend. Voices are drawn from letters, invitations, men's public reflections and approved testimonials only, each with a release on file. Until they arrive, the organisation's own lines and Dorian's words carry the pages.
Numbers
$320, ages 12 to 17, 11 to 13 September 2026, the stops, since 1990. Nothing else unless the Society can source it and stand behind it; the site does not count the weekends.
Language
Young men, never boys. Production men, not staff. The weekend, not the event. Rite of passage, not program.

08 · Sharing it

How a young man hears about this in the first place.

Most young men arrive because a man they knew told their parents. Everything below is built to make that one conversation easier to start and harder to forget.

The goal Dorian named is 50 young men registered. With nine days, that number moves one way: through the men, each carrying his own link into conversations he is already having, and through families forwarding one honest message. Those two levers come first below and are marked for the nine days. Everything else is the spring, kept here so the site built today does not have to be undone to make room for it.

Among the men

  • Nine daysWho brought you? Who will you bring? Every registered young man's family and every production man gets a personal link. Not a discount, an invitation with his name on it. The team sees, privately, which invitations became registrations. The written invitations are also the first source Voices draws on.
  • Nine daysThe men's own link. Each production man carries a QR card in his wallet. At the every-other-Thursday meeting the count is read out, kindly. Men are competitive about the right things.
  • Nine daysThe alumni line. Men who came as young men are asked for one written line for the walk out. Now that men may be named, their names go on the site if they want them there.

For families

  • Nine daysSend this to a dad. One tap writes the text message and attaches the clip. The message is honest: "This is the weekend I wish I'd had."
  • Nine daysThe field card in the world. The printable card doubles as the flyer: a school counsellor pins it, a coach hands it out, a barbershop keeps a stack by the register.
  • SpringThe Open Fire. One of the monthly gatherings the communications strategy already plans becomes the spring enrolment night: an actual fire, in Burnaby or Squamish, families and young men invited, men who have been there speaking, RSVP on the site.

Partners

  • SpringThe Partners page and kit. Youth organisations, treatment centres, faith communities and schools, exactly the audiences the partner email templates already address: the 20-minute meeting ask, a one-page PDF and a 60-second film they can forward.
  • OpenWork-to-earn with a partner. A community organisation provides the service hours; the Society provides the seat. The story writes itself and it is true, but whether work-to-earn runs in 2026 is not confirmed, so the site does not offer it until Dorian says so.

In public

  • Nine daysThe bus-stop posters. QR posters in the three pickup towns where young men actually are: climbing gyms, skate parks, barbershops, rec centres. The poster is a photograph and eight words; it can be printed the day the domain resolves.
  • SpringThe countdown films. From next year's kickoff to September, one vertical clip a week on Instagram and Facebook, cut from the real footage, captioned, no faces without releases. Twelve weeks out, the countdown starts counting.
  • SpringLocal press. A volunteer society of men running a rite of passage since 1990 is a story the Squamish and Burnaby papers will print, especially with consented photographs from September. The "What he built" page is the hook, when it exists.
  • SpringSearch. If the Society qualifies for Google for Nonprofits, the Ad Grants program funds search advertising at no cost; worth one afternoon to find out. Either way the site is written so that the searches parents actually type ("rite of passage BC", "youth mentorship Squamish", the plain words for a wilderness weekend for teenagers) land on it.
  • SpringThe two newsletters. Rising the Man Within for families and The Forged Circle for the men, already designed on paper in the communications strategy, get a home and a sign-up on the site.

A yearly rhythm, from spring on

Dorian's word for the spring is "potentially", and the rhythm below is held that lightly. Kickoff opens enrolment. Spring Open Fire. Summer countdown films. September weekend and capture. October "What he built" gathering and the press pitch. Then the site turns to next year, with this year's consented footage in it. The site is never finished; it grows a ring every September.

01 · The idea

Make the site do to a visitor what the weekend does to a young man.

The hero's journey has three moves: separation, trial, return. A young man arriving at ymaw.com on his phone, sent there by his father, is at the first move whether he knows it or not. The site should feel like that. Not a camp brochure with a price at the bottom, but a place that takes him seriously, shows him the real thing, asks him one honest question, and ends with an action he takes himself.

The current site is good and it is honest. It is also written from the parent's side of the screen, and it is built on a mix of real footage and animated photographs. The reimagined site keeps what works (the scroll-driven trail, the field-guide palette, the standards, the "young men, never boys" discipline) and changes three things at the root:

  • Two doors at the front. "I'm 12 to 17" and "I'm bringing him." Same weekend, two voices, one registration. The young man is never talked about in the third person on his own path.
  • Every frame is real. No animated stills, no stock, no AI. What is on the site happened at a YMAW weekend. This is the one thing a young man can smell from a mile off, and the one thing no other youth program's website in BC can claim.
  • The site crosses into the weekend. A line he writes on the site in September comes back to him, on paper, at Future Plans on Sunday. The website is the first station and the bus is the second.

We aren't a summer camp; we're a rite of passage.

YMAW enrolment call script

What "award-winning" actually takes, stripped of the hype: one clear idea executed all the way down, typography and motion that belong to the subject, real material, and a page that loads fast and works for everyone. Sites that win do one thing nobody else could do. Ours is that everything on it is true.

02 · Six rules

The standards the site is held to, the way the men are held to theirs.

  1. Real over rendered. Photographs and film from YMAW weekends only. Where we have no footage of something, we write about it or we film it in September. We do not generate it.
  2. His voice and theirs. Every page speaks to the young man in the second person somewhere, and to his family in the plain, unhurried voice a good production man uses at the bus stop.
  3. Nothing invented. No statistics we cannot source, no quotes nobody said, no promises the weekend does not keep. If it is on the site, a man on the team can stand behind it in a parking lot.
  4. Consent is the design constraint. No identifiable young man appears without a signed release from a guardian and his own assent. The site is designed so that it is beautiful with backs, hands, silhouettes and landscape, and becomes richer as consents arrive.
  5. The sacred stays off camera. The Push, the Sunday ceremony and anything said in a circle are never filmed, recorded or described beyond what the handbook already says publicly.
  6. Fast, quiet, for everyone. Loads in under two seconds on a phone on a bus. Works without sound, without motion, without JavaScript, with a screen reader. Sound and motion are gifts you opt into, not a toll.

03 · The trail

Ten places, one path.

The site map below is a trail, not a menu. Every page hands you to the next one, and every page ends at the same fire: registration. The names are working names.

The Threshold
Home. Two doors, then one continuous scroll through the weekend in real footage: dusk, dawn, the Quests, the circle, Sunday, the walk out. The version live today is the prototype of this.
His Path
The young man's door. Second person throughout. What actually happens, what is hard, what nobody makes you do, what you come home with. Ends with "Write your line" and Register.
Bringing Him
The family's door. Why this exists, safety and the standards, what $320 covers, how the weekend runs hour by hour, what to expect Sunday night. Ends with Register and Come as a Sponsor.
The Weekend
The field log, hour by hour, each hour with a real clip or still from that hour. The one page both doors share.
Voices
Short vertical films and written lines from young men and men, every one with a signed release. Empty until the 2026 weekend gives us consented material; launched the day it does.
Since 1990
The archive. One photograph per year we have (the "Old" album, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2019, 2024, 2026), scrubbed through time. "Then and now": men who came as young men.
The Men
Who runs it, the departments, the standards, the Man Code, how to join. The straight-talk block for men stays.
Register
One form, three roles, five minutes on a phone. Card, Apple Pay and Google Pay when Stripe is unpaused; e-transfer; the assistance and work-to-earn box. The confirmation is the walk out.
Support
Send a young man, sponsor one you know, give time on the load and strike crews, spread the word. Donation receipts if the Society can issue them.
Partners
For schools, counsellors, youth organisations, treatment centres and faith communities: the 20-minute meeting ask, the work-to-earn path, a downloadable kit. This page exists because the outreach templates already do.

Two more pages appear only when they are useful: While He's Gone, a page for parents to read over the weekend itself, and Events, the monthly gatherings and the January kickoff that the communications strategy already describes.

04 · Signature moments

The ten things a visitor remembers.

Each moment says what it is, why it matters to a young man, and what it needs from us. Most need footage and consent more than they need code.

Real, not rendered

Dusk mode

The site knows the visitor's local time. After sunset the paper goes to night and the fire is the only warm light on the page. Friday, dusk, on his phone, at dusk.

Needs: nothing but code and the night footage we have.

The fire

The hearth on the home page is the real Saturday-night fire. Press and hold to hear it crackle. Sound is opt-in, recorded at the weekend, never music.

Needs: a clean 60 s audio capture in September.

The bus countdown

Days, hours, minutes to the Friday pickup, with the three stops and the Island carpool on a hand-drawn map. When it hits zero it becomes While He's Gone.

Needs: confirmed stop locations and times.

Letter to Sunday

At registration the young man may write one line to himself. The team prints it and hands it to him at Future Plans. The site reaches into the weekend and the weekend reaches back.

Needs: the PTM's yes, a printer, an envelope per young man.

The field card

Registration produces a printable card: the packing list, the arc of the weekend, and a blank line for the standard he will write on Friday night. Something to hold on the bus.

Needs: the packing list and a one-hour design pass.

The walk out

The registration confirmation is the two lines of men, in photographs, and one written line from a man who was once on the bus. He has been received before he has left home.

Needs: releases from a dozen production men and one true paragraph.

Voices

Vertical films of 30 to 60 seconds, captioned, no music, shot at a "voices booth" on Sunday afternoon with three questions. Young men with releases, men with releases.

Needs: consent forms at the bus stop, a phone on a tripod, one man for two hours.

Since 1990

A timeline you scrub with your thumb. One frame per year we have, from the old prints to last September. Faces in the old photographs only with the men's permission; they are men now.

Needs: a date for every album and an hour with Dorian on names.

Send this to a dad

Every page has one share button that writes the message for you: a real line from the page, a vertical clip, the link. Built for a text message, because that is how young men arrive.

Needs: the 9:16 cuts we already know how to make.

What he built

Four weeks after the weekend, a page for families and young men: the consented photographs from their year, and an invitation to the October gathering. The trail does not end at the bus stop.

Needs: the documentarian's work from September and a gathering date.

What is deliberately not on this list: parallax for its own sake, gamified quizzes, badges, a chatbot, anything that asks a young man for data the weekend does not need.

05 · Content

Two voices, one truth, written new.

All copy is rewritten from the source documents (The Big Why, the standards, the flyer, the partner templates, the communications strategy) and from Dorian's answers to the questions at the end. Two registers:

To him
Second person, present tense, short sentences, no adjectives that sell. Says what is hard first. Never explains what he should feel. "Your phone spends the weekend in your bag. Everyone survives this, including you."
To them
Plain, unhurried, specific. Safety and standards before magic. Names what $320 covers to the meal. Ends every page with the one thing they can do next.
Quotes
Only recorded words with a signed release, gathered at the voices booth in September and from men who write their own line for the walk out. Until then, the organisation's own lines carry the pages.
Numbers
Price, ages, dates, stops, since 1990. Nothing else unless the Society can source it and stand behind it.
Language
Young men, never boys. Production men, not staff. The weekend, not the event. Rite of passage, not program.

A content matrix (page by audience by job) comes with the first draft, so every paragraph can be traced to a reader and a reason. Reading level for His Path is checked against a 13-year-old, not a 40-year-old.

Needs a release before it goes live

Every interview, every close-up, the boxing pads with a face, the line of young men facing camera, anything a classmate could recognise. The 2024 interviews (seven young men, two men) are the best material in the archive and they need a guardian's signature each. Dorian knows these families.

The September capture

The weekend in nine days is the single biggest opportunity this plan has, and it costs one man's weekend. Proposal: appoint one production man as Documentarian, a real role on the team chart, with a shot list mapped to the ten moments above and a rule that he never films a circle. Consent forms go out with the pickup details and are signed at the bus stop; a young man who opts out wears a coloured wristband and the documentarian works around him. Deliverables to aim for:

  • Twenty stills that carry a page each: the bus at dusk, the hike in as silhouettes, hands (knots, axe, fire), the shelters, a Quest from behind, the kitchen, the Sunday game wide, the walk out from behind the lines.
  • Twelve clips of five to ten seconds, tripod or steady, each one a moment on the trail, shot for both 16:9 and 9:16.
  • Six sound captures of one minute: fire, the drum circle, the bus, wind on the beach, the game, morning birds.
  • Eight voices at the Sunday booth: five young men, three men, three questions each, releases signed.

Hosting: the footage moves off the temporary CDN it sits on now to a video host the Society owns and pays for (Mux or Cloudflare Stream, roughly the price of two coffees a month at this traffic), with the originals kept in the Society's Drive.

07 · The build

Keep the engine, replace everything on it.

Experience
The scroll-driven trail (the scrollcraft engine already running) stays as the spine of the home page and The Weekend; it is proven on desktop and phone. Every other page is plain, fast HTML.
Identity
The logo's colours on warm paper stay unless Dorian wants a reset (question 1). Typography gets a real display face for headlines and a hyper-legible body face for a 13-year-old on a cracked phone screen.
Registration
Supabase for the records, owner-only access, no third-party trackers. Stripe with Apple Pay and Google Pay when unpaused; e-transfer with a reference code as it works today; the assistance and work-to-earn box; the Google Sheet sync the team asked for.
Consent
Releases collected at the bus stop on paper and photographed into the registration record, or signed on a phone through the same form. A "faces list" the site checks before any identifiable image is published.
Performance
Budget: first paint under two seconds on a 4G phone, under five megabytes per page view including the first clip, reduced-motion visitors get stills, no sound without a tap.
Accessibility
WCAG AA contrast on both grounds, captions on every voice, keyboard through everything, alt text written by a person.
Domain
ymaw.com points at the new site on launch day with the old registration links redirected; the .vercel.app address is never printed anywhere.

08 · Sharing it

How a young man hears about this in the first place.

Most young men arrive because a man they knew told their parents. Everything below is built to make that one conversation easier to start and harder to forget.

Inside the circle

  • Who brought you? Who will you bring? Every registered young man's family and every production man gets a personal link. Not a discount, an invitation with his name on it. The team sees, privately, which invitations became registrations.
  • The men's own link. Each production man carries a QR card in his wallet. At the every-other-Thursday meeting the count is read out, kindly. Men are competitive about the right things.
  • The alumni line. Men who came as young men are asked for one written line for the walk out and one for the October gathering. Their names go on the site only if they want them there.

For families

  • Send this to a dad. One tap writes the text message and attaches the clip. The message is honest: "This is the weekend I wish I'd had."
  • The field card in the world. The printable card doubles as the flyer: a school counsellor pins it, a coach hands it out, a barbershop keeps a stack by the register.
  • The Open Fire. One of the monthly gatherings the communications strategy already plans becomes the spring enrolment night: an actual fire, in Burnaby or Squamish, families and young men invited, men who have been there speaking, RSVP on the site.

Partners

  • The Partners page and kit. Youth organisations, treatment centres, faith communities and schools, exactly the audiences the partner email templates already address: the 20-minute meeting ask, the work-to-earn path, a one-page PDF and a 60-second film they can forward.
  • Work-to-earn with a partner. A community organisation provides the service hours; the Society provides the seat. The story writes itself and it is true.

In public

  • The bus-stop posters. QR posters in the three pickup towns where young men actually are: climbing gyms, skate parks, barbershops, rec centres. The poster is a photograph and eight words.
  • The countdown films. From the January kickoff to September, one vertical clip a week on Instagram and Facebook, cut from the real footage, captioned, no faces without releases. Twelve weeks out, the countdown starts counting.
  • Local press. A volunteer society of men running a rite of passage since 1990 is a story the Squamish and Burnaby papers will print, especially with consented photographs from September. Pitch in October with the "What he built" page as the hook.
  • Search. If the Society qualifies for Google for Nonprofits, the Ad Grants program funds search advertising at no cost; worth one afternoon to find out. Either way the site is written so that "rite of passage BC", "boys wilderness weekend BC" and "youth mentorship Squamish" land on it.
  • The two newsletters. Rising the Man Within for families and The Forged Circle for the men, already designed on paper in the communications strategy, get a home and a sign-up on the site.

A yearly rhythm

January kickoff opens enrolment. Spring Open Fire. Summer countdown films. September weekend and capture. October "What he built" gathering and the press pitch. Then the site turns to next year, with this year's consented footage in it. The site is never finished; it grows a ring every September.

    AssumedRoles only until you say otherwise.

  6. The founding story.

    Who started this in 1990, where, and why? Five sentences from you, in your words, become the spine of Since 1990. Is 2026 the 34th annual weekend, as the flyer says?

  7. Facts to lock.

    $320 flat for everyone, or early bird with a later price? May the site name the actual location, or only "Squamish region"? Exact bus stops and times. Phones in bags: rule or custom? Is work-to-earn running in 2026? Is "non-denominational" the right word?

  11. Accounts and the domain.

  12. Payments.

    A number of young men on the bus by a date, a number of sponsors, a number of partner organisations, or something else? The site is designed toward whatever you name.

    How many hours a week can you give this through October? Is there a filmmaker, photographer or designer among the men who wants a real job on this?

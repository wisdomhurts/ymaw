The Weekend · September 11–13, 2026

Three days. One journey. Nobody comes back the same.

This is not a summer camp. It is a rite of passage, built on the shape of the hero's journey: he leaves what he knows, enters a world of men, is tested, and returns. Here is what that looks like, hour by hour, so you know exactly where he is and why. He should read it too.

  • Sept 11–13Friday evening to Sunday afternoon
  • Squamish regionWilderness site, BC
  • Ages 12–17Young men of every background
  • $320Per person. Everyone pays the same.
  • Since 1990Volunteer men. Nobody is paid.

Straight talk

If you're the young man, this part is yours.

Your parents get the rest of the page. Here is the weekend with nothing polished off it.

We believe in you. Now prove it to yourself.

YMAW
  • You ride a bus north with people you have never met, then hike into camp in the dark carrying your own gear. That is not a mistake in the plan. That is the plan.
  • Your phone spends the weekend in your bag. Everyone survives this, including you.
  • You build the shelter you sleep in, with a team that did not exist an hour earlier, and your team picks who leads it.
  • Saturday you do things you did not think you could, and some things you fail at. Both count. Nobody finishes a Quest alone, and nobody gets carried.
  • Saturday night the men go first. Nobody makes you say anything. Nobody performs. What is said in the circle stays in the circle.
  • Sunday you take on a field of grown men in a ball game with almost no rules. It is loud.
  • You come home tired, dirty, and carrying yourself differently, with a plan for the year that you wrote, not anyone else.

The arc

Separation. Trial. Return.

It begins with separation from the known: home, friends, routine. He boards the bus with young men he has never met and rides north into the dark. On Friday evening he enters the world of men. He is placed on a team, builds his own camp, competes, and helps write the standards the whole weekend will live by.

Saturday is the trial. The Quest Stations test body and mind from morning until the light goes. Then the focus turns inward. In The Push he is invited to set down what he has been carrying and find the resilience that was in him all along.

Sunday is the return. A ceremony, a plan for the year ahead written in his own hand, one last wild game against the men, and the walk out between two lines of the men who witnessed his weekend. Then the bus home.

Hour by hour

The field log.

Every weekend brings its own quests. The shape below is how past weekends have run. The production team follows the energy of the young men and adjusts in real time, because the real journey happens in real time.

FridayEvening

The bus, the dark, the hike in

Pickups in Burnaby, Langley and Squamish. The bus reaches the site around dusk and the young men hike in carrying their own gear, usually in the dark. It is physical work and it should feel a little risky. That is the point: he has left the known world.

Dinner. Then everyone lines up by height, and the pecking order every group of young men carries in with them gets sized up in the open, once, and set aside. Teams are formed and each team chooses a leader. The new team heads off to build its own shelter, the first place leadership shows.

Late, the teams circle up for hot chocolate. Later still comes the Friday Night Riot, when the young men get to blow off steam, often into the small hours.

SaturdayDawn to dusk

The Quest Stations

Up early. Morning circle clears anything left over from the night and gets bodies moving. Breakfast. Then the big circle, where the day's directions are given with every young man and every man present.

From mid-morning the teams move between Quest Stations built by the production team: challenges of strength, nerve, problem-solving and trust that no one can finish alone. Lunch happens out at the stations. The quest runs into the afternoon, then there is time off, the summer-camp part of the weekend: games, water, food, and a team that has started to feel like one.

SaturdayNight

The Push

After dinner the focus shifts from physical and mental effort to emotional depth. The men talk first. Then, in The Push, the young men are invited to empty what they have been carrying and discover the resilience that has been in them all along. It is held in a circle, by men who go first, with every YMAW standard in force. No one is made to speak. Many do.

Lights out for the young men. The production team meets afterward to check on every one of them.

SundayMorning

Ceremony and Future Plans

Morning circle, breakfast, and the whole camp together one more time. A ceremony marks what has changed. Then each young man writes his Future Plans: what he is taking home and what he will do with it in the year ahead. His words, his plan.

Then the young men take on the men in a wild, rowdy ball game, part handball, part British Bulldog. It is their chance to beat up on the men, and it is loud.

SundayAfternoon

The walk out

The young men strike their camps and pack. Bag lunch. When they leave the site they walk between two lines of the production men, eye to eye with every man they just spent the weekend with, witnessed by their Shadow Watchers. Then the bus, and home to you.

Expect him to be tired, dirty, and quiet in a different way than when he left. Give it a day. Then ask him what he built.

What $320 covers

Everything he needs from the bus stop back to the bus stop.

  • Two nights and three days of fun, adventure and challenge
  • Transport from Burnaby, Langley or Squamish, and a carpool from Vancouver Island
  • Meals: snacks Friday; breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks Saturday; breakfast and lunch Sunday
  • Accommodation: camping, in shelters the teams build (a packing list comes with registration)
  • A screened, trained volunteer production team; no man is ever alone with a young man
  • Outrageous fun, adventure, support and meaning

Everyone at the weekend pays the same $320: young men, sponsors and production men alike. If money is the obstacle, register anyway and tick the assistance box. A man from the team will call you privately and work it out. No young man misses the weekend over money.

Safety and standards

The container he is held in.

The weekend runs on a written set of standards that every man on the production team commits to before he is allowed on site, and that the young men add to on Friday night. The ones a parent asks about first:

  • Buddy system. No one is ever alone. Every man is visible to at least one other man at all times, and no man is ever alone with a young man.
  • Safety call. Anyone, of any age, can call "safety". When it is called, everything stops and everyone follows directions.
  • No drugs, alcohol or tobacco at the weekend or in the 24 hours before any YMAW event.
  • Confidentiality. What is seen and heard stays in the circle. He can tell you what he learned; he will not tell you another young man's story, and no man will either.
  • We say "young men". Every man on the team refers to the youth as young men. It sets the bar for how they are treated and how they treat each other.
  • Fathers trust the process. A father who staffs the weekend lets the team hold his son. This is his son's time.

Every man on staff is screened, and every first-time staffer shadows before he leads.

YMAW production standards

Production men complete a screening and a Friday-morning training before the young men arrive. Medical information you give at registration is kept private and used only to look after him. The media release you sign at registration decides whether he may appear in YMAW's photos and video; his name is never published without separate permission, and you can withdraw the release at any time by emailing info@ymaw.com.

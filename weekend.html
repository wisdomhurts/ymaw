The Weekend · September 11–13, 2026

Three days. One journey. He comes home changed.

The Young Men's Adventure Weekend follows the shape of the hero's journey: he leaves what he knows, enters a world of men, is tested, and returns. Here is what that looks like, hour by hour, so you know exactly where he is and why.

  • Sept 11–13Friday evening to Sunday afternoon
  • Squamish regionWilderness site, BC
  • Ages 12–17Young men of every background
  • $320Per person. Everyone pays the same.
  • Since 1990Run by volunteer men

The arc

Separation. Trial. Return.

It begins with separation from the known: home, friends, routine. He boards the bus with young men he has never met and rides north into the dark. On Friday evening he enters the world of men. He is placed on a team, builds his own camp, competes, and helps write the standards the whole weekend will live by.

Saturday is the trial. The Quest Stations test body and mind from morning until the light goes. Then the focus turns inward. In The Push he is invited to set down what he has been carrying and find the resilience that was in him all along.

Sunday is the return. A ceremony, a plan for the year ahead, one last wild game against the men, and the walk through two lines of the men who witnessed his weekend. Then the bus home.

Hour by hour

The field log.

Every weekend brings its own quests. The shape below is how past weekends have run; the production team follows the energy of the young men and adjusts in real time.

FridayEvening

The bus, the dark, the hike in

Pickups in Burnaby, Langley and Squamish. The bus arrives at the site around dusk and the young men hike in carrying their own gear, usually in the dark. It is physical work and it should feel a little risky. That is the point: he has left the known world.

Dinner. Then everyone lines up by height, and the pecking order every group of young men carries in with them gets sized up in the open. Teams are formed and a leader is chosen. That new team heads off to build its own shelter together, the first chance for leadership to show.

Late, the teams circle up for hot chocolate. Later still comes the Friday Night Riot, when the young men are allowed to blow off steam, often into the small hours.

SaturdayDawn to dusk

The Quest Stations

Up early. Morning circle clears anything left over from the night and gets bodies moving. Breakfast. Then the big circle, where the day's directions are given with every young man and every man present.

From mid-morning the teams move between Quest Stations built by the production team: challenges of strength, nerve, problem-solving and trust that no one can finish alone. Lunch happens out at the stations. The quest runs into the afternoon, then there is time off, the summer-camp part of the weekend: fun and camaraderie with no agenda.

SaturdayNight

The Push

After dinner the focus shifts from physical and mental effort to emotional depth. The men talk first. Then, in The Push, the young men are invited to empty what they have been carrying and discover the resilience that has been in them all along. It is held in a circle, by men who have done their own work, with every YMAW standard in force.

Lights out for the young men. The production team meets afterward to check on every one of them.

SundayMorning

Ceremony and future plans

Morning circle, breakfast, and the whole camp together one more time. A ceremony marks what has changed. Then each young man makes his Future Plans: what he is taking home and what he will do with it in the year ahead.

Then the young men take on the men in a wild, rowdy ball game, part handball, part British Bulldog. It is their chance to beat up on the men, and it is loud.

SundayAfternoon

The walk out

The young men strike their camps and pack. Bag lunch. When they leave the site they walk between two lines of the production men, eye to eye with every man they just spent the weekend with, witnessed by their Shadow Watchers. Then the bus, and home to you.

Expect him to be tired, dirty, and quiet in a different way than when he left.

What $320 covers

Everything he needs from the bus stop back to the bus stop.

  • Two nights and three days of fun, adventure and challenge
  • Transport from Burnaby, Langley or Squamish, and a carpool from Vancouver Island
  • Meals: snacks Friday; breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks Saturday; breakfast and lunch Sunday
  • Accommodation: camping, in shelters the teams build (a packing list comes with registration)
  • A screened, trained volunteer production team, never fewer than two men with any young man
  • Outrageous fun, adventure, support and meaning

Everyone at the weekend pays the same $320: young men, sponsors and production men alike. If money is the thing standing in the way, ask for assistance on the registration form. We have never turned a young man away for it.

Safety and standards

The container he is held in.

The weekend runs on a written set of standards that every man on the production team commits to before he is allowed on site, and that the young men add to on Friday night. The ones a parent asks about first:

  • Buddy system. No one is ever alone. Every man is visible to at least one other man at all times, and no man is ever alone with a young man.
  • Safety call. Anyone, of any age, can call "safety". When it is called, everything stops and everyone follows directions.
  • No drugs, alcohol or tobacco at the weekend or in the 24 hours before any YMAW event.
  • Confidentiality. What is seen and heard stays in the circle. He can tell you what he learned; he will not tell you another young man's story, and no man will either.
  • Young men, not boys. The organization refers to the youth as young men. It sets the bar for how they are treated and how they treat each other.
  • Fathers trust the process. A father who staffs the weekend lets the team hold his son. This is his son's time.

Every man on staff is screened, and every first-time staffer shadows before he leads.

YMAW production standards

Production men complete a screening and a Friday-morning training on site before the young men arrive. Medical information you give at registration travels with the safety lead all weekend and nowhere else.

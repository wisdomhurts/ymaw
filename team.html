The Team

Men who show up. Every year since 1990.

Nobody at YMAW is paid. The weekend is planned, built, cooked, driven, guarded and held by a volunteer production team of men who pay the same $320 as the young men and arrive a day early to train. This is who they are, what it asks of a man, and how to become one of them.

How it works

A production, not a camp.

We call the staff the production team because that is what the weekend is: a production, planned for months and run on a schedule to the minute, that exists to create the conditions for a young man's hero's journey. The team meets every other Thursday evening in Burnaby, and by video on the off weeks, with men joining from Alberta to Washington State.

Each department has a leader and a second, reporting to the managers. At the top, the Production Team Manager and the S1, his second-in-command, oversee the whole operation. In short: follow the lead of the S1, and take your questions to your team leader.

Your first weekend, you shadow. You walk alongside the young men in an observational role, and by Sunday you understand how the event happens. Nobody leads before he has shadowed.

Straight talk

What it asks of you.

You will be challenged mentally, physically, emotionally and, if you let it, spiritually. You will sleep less than you want to and carry more than you planned to. You will watch a young man do something on Saturday that he could not do on Friday, and you will know you were part of the container that made it possible. Men come back for that.

No one is immune to the crucible of transformation and initiation.

The Big Why, YMAW production handbook
  • Come as you are. First time or twentieth, you will be well supported and ready.
  • Show up on time, sober, prepared, and sworn to the standards. The young men are watching what you do, not what you say.
  • Be prepared to stretch your own limiting beliefs and habits. The weekend works on the men too.
  • Fathers: trust the process and let the team hold your son. This is his time. Yours comes in the circle with the other men.

Departments

Where a man can serve.

Some men belong to more than one team. Every role is real work, and every role is in front of the young men, who learn more from what we do than from what we say.

Management

The Production Team Manager and the S1 carry the whole weekend, from enrolling the key managers six months out to the final report to the board.

Shadow Watchers

Men who walk alongside the young men all weekend, witness their journey, and stand in the closing ceremony. Every first-timer starts here.

Quest Stations

Design and run Saturday's challenges of strength, nerve, problem-solving and trust. Set up while the young men play, then hold the station all day.

Site

Prep the site the week before, build the camp infrastructure, honour the site all weekend, and strike it Sunday afternoon.

Kitchen

Feed the whole camp from Friday snacks to Sunday lunch, in the field, on time.

Transport

The bus, the pickups in Burnaby, Langley and Squamish, the Vancouver Island carpool, and the truck that carries everything in and out.

Safety

Safety practice before the young men arrive, the medical information from registration, and the authority to stop everything when "safety" is called.

Load and strike

Can't make the weekend? Load the truck the Thursday before, or meet the truck Sunday evening in Abbotsford to unload it.

The standards

What every production man commits to.

The young men live by a set of standards for the weekend. The men hold those and more. Some of them:

  • Safety first. If you see something unsafe, take charge and communicate it.
  • Never alone. Every man is visible to at least one other man at all times, and never alone with a young man.
  • No drugs, alcohol or tobacco at YMAW or in the 24 hours before any YMAW event.
  • Honour the site. Leave things better than you found them.
  • Confidentiality. Speak to your own experience only. Other men's words and names stay in the circle.
  • Be on time, ready. No foul language. Refer to the youth as young men.
  • Don't make anything up. Get clear direction, set your ego aside, and tell your manager if you have misled anyone.
  • Supportability. Trust the team. You are not being supportive if everyone else is wrong.
  • Fathers of young men: trust the process. Let the team hold your son. This is his time.
  • Clean it up. When you find yourself out of integrity, call yourself back in with a simple act of service.

Join the production team

Come stand shoulder to shoulder with other men in service.

Give a young man the experience you wish you had growing up, and take your own initiation weekend alongside him. Whether it is your first staffing or your twentieth, you will be well supported and ready.

  • Screening. Every production man completes a screening before he is on site with young men.
  • Training. Friday morning, September 11, in Squamish, before the bus arrives: safety practice, the standards, your role.
  • Meetings. Every other Thursday in Burnaby, and by video on the off weeks, from now until the weekend.
  • Cost. $320, the same as everyone. Ask for assistance if you need it.
